# Pacely Athlete Training Network

Build and deploy a working web application from this brief. There is no starting codebase. When you are done, a stranger must be able to open the app in a browser, sign in, upload a GPX run or ride, and see it turned into distance, moving time, pace, kilometre splits and climbing, see its efforts ranked on segment leaderboards with personal records, see it appear at the top of the feeds of the athletes who follow them where those athletes give kudos and comment, and see it advance the athlete's monthly challenge progress, without hitting an error page. Every figure is computed and stored on the server: numbers that only appear on screen while the stored activity disagrees do not count.

## Problem and users

Pacely is an athlete training network, taking no real card payment, for amateur runners, riders, walkers and hikers who want motivation from a community: "Community-Powered Motivation" and "Record. Sweat. Share. Kudos." An athlete uploads the GPX file their watch recorded; the server parses it into an activity with exact distance, moving and elapsed time, pace, kilometre splits, best efforts and elevation gain, matches it against segments (named stretches of road or trail) and ranks it on their leaderboards, and posts it to the followers feed. Followers give kudos and comment, private accounts approve their followers, a privacy zone hides the area around an athlete's home, and monthly challenges fill up as activities count toward them. Each activity page replays the route as an animated flyover, reads its splits aloud, and each athlete can play back a music-backed recap of their year. The public face is a marketing site: the signup home page, the subscription feature tour, a map explorer, the challenges gallery, the plans for individuals, families, students and teams, the company story and product news.

| Role | Can do |
|---|---|
| Visitor | Reads the public pages, browses challenges and plans, uses the team price calculator, signs up or logs in. **Cannot** read any activity, feed, segment leaderboard, athlete profile or stat without signing in. |
| Athlete | Uploads, edits and deletes their own activities; follows athletes; gives kudos and comments on activities they can see; creates segments from their own activities; joins challenges; sets their privacy and time zone; chooses a plan; deletes their own account. Sees the top ten entries of any segment leaderboard plus their own entry. **Cannot** read another athlete's activity that is hidden from them, edit or delete another athlete's activity, give kudos to their own activity, or delete another athlete's comment on someone else's activity. |
| Subscriber | Everything an athlete can do, and sees every entry of a segment leaderboard. |
| Private athlete | An athlete whose account is private: a new follower waits until they approve, and only approved followers see their activities. |

Authorization is enforced **server-side on every request**. A hidden activity answers `404` to everyone but its owner, whatever the route. There is no administrator back door.

Rules that must always hold:

1. An athlete who requests an activity hidden from them, whether `only_me`, `followers` without an accepted follow, or a private athlete's activity without an accepted follow, is answered as not found on every activity route (read, streams, kudos, comments and export), and the activity, its kudos and its comments are left unchanged.
2. Only the owner may edit, delete or export an activity; anyone else is refused and nothing changes.
3. Kudos on your own activity is refused, and a comment may be deleted only by its author or the activity owner; anyone else is refused.
4. Each athlete holds at most one activity per recording, one kudos per activity and one effort per activity on each segment.
5. Another athlete's email address never appears in any response, and no response ever carries a password or a password hash.
6. A deleted account leaves nothing behind that another athlete can see or count.

## Journeys

**Sign up and upload.** A visitor lands on the home page under the headline "Community-Powered Motivation", presses "Sign Up With Email", and signs up with a name, email and password, arriving on the dashboard with their feed and a twelve-week chart of their training. The athlete opens Upload, chooses a GPX file, sees a preview of the parsed distance, sets the name, sport and visibility, and presses "Save activity". The new activity page opens with the distance, moving time, pace and elevation gain, the route on the map and the splits table. They press "Play flyover" and watch a marker travel the route, step along the elevation profile with the arrow keys, and press "Play audio splits" to hear each kilometre read aloud.

**Segments and the feed.** The athlete marks a stretch of the activity as a new segment. A friend who follows them uploads a faster run through the same stretch, sees a crown on that effort, and finds the activity's author second on the segment leaderboard. The friend opens their feed, sees the athlete's activity at the top, presses the kudos button and leaves a comment; the athlete sees the kudos count rise and the comment appear.

**Challenges, recap, privacy and plans.** The athlete joins the "Monthly 5K Challenge" on the challenges page and watches its progress ring fill from the activities they uploaded this month. They open their recap, press "Play recap", and the cards advance over music. In settings they make their account private and set a privacy zone around their home, and a new follower then waits for approval. On the plans page they switch between annual and monthly prices and start a trial. They log out from the avatar menu and return to the home page.

**Definition of done.** A stranger signs up, uploads a GPX activity and sees the exact figures the parsing rules produce; the activity ranks on segment leaderboards with personal records and reaches followers' feeds for kudos and comments; monthly challenges, weekly stats and the recap reflect it. A hidden activity or privacy-zone point never reaches anyone else, and the look described in Visual design holds on a phone, by keyboard and under reduced motion.

## Mandatory requirements

- **DP-01 — Sign up with email.** `POST /api/auth/signup` with a valid email, a name of one to eighty characters and a password of at least eight characters returns `201` with a working token, refuses invalid fields with `400` naming each one and a registered email with `409` `email_taken`, and the signup page marks invalid fields inline.
- **DP-02 — Log in and log out.** `POST /api/auth/login` returns `200` with a token or `401` for a wrong password, `POST /api/auth/logout` ends that token, and the login page shows the mismatch message for a wrong password.
- **DP-03 — Seeded athletes.** The four seeded athletes sign in with the seeded password and are stored in the `athletes` collection, with Ava Brooks a subscriber on the `individual` plan.
- **DP-04 — Time zone setting.** A new athlete's `time_zone` is `UTC`, and `PATCH /api/me` stores an IANA zone name and refuses any other name with `400` naming `time_zone`.
- **DP-05 — Account deletion.** `DELETE /api/me` with the right password erases the account at once so its email no longer logs in and may sign up again as a new athlete, while a wrong or missing password answers `403` `wrong_password` and changes nothing.
- **DP-06 — GPX parsing.** An uploaded GPX activity returns and stores the distance, elapsed time, moving time, elevation gain, pace or speed and UTC start time that the parsing rules produce, including in a request body of 4500000 bytes.
- **DP-07 — Stored activity.** A valid upload is stored as one row in `activities`, takes the athlete's default visibility when none is sent and tops the athlete's own feed, and athlete activity lists need a session.
- **DP-08 — Upload page.** The upload page previews the parsed distance, saves the activity and opens its page, and the upload page, the new activity page and the dashboard meet the accessibility, touch target, font and reflow rules.
- **DP-09 — Kilometre splits.** Splits interpolate each whole kilometre crossing and end with a final split for a remainder of at least one metre, and the splits table marks the fastest whole kilometre with Fastest.
- **DP-10 — Best efforts.** A run lists its `400m`, `1k` and `5k` best efforts as the fastest point-to-point windows, and rides, walks and hikes have empty `best_efforts`.
- **DP-11 — Streams.** `GET /api/activities/{id}/streams` returns four arrays with one entry per point and needs a session.
- **DP-12 — Audio splits.** Play audio splits speaks one utterance per split, in order, through the browser's speech synthesis.
- **DP-13 — Activity page.** The activity page shows its figures, route, controls and medals with their text alternatives, and meets the accessibility, touch target and font rules.
- **DP-14 — Refused uploads.** A malformed or single-point GPX, an invalid name, sport or visibility, or an upload without a session is refused with nothing stored, and the upload page shows the reason without leaving the page.
- **DP-15 — One activity per recording.** A second upload with the same first-point time answers `409` `duplicate_activity` naming the existing activity, and three identical simultaneous uploads store exactly one.
- **DP-16 — Idempotent upload.** A replay with the same `Idempotency-Key` and body returns the same activity id without a second activity, and the same key with a new body answers `422` `idempotency_key_reused`.
- **DP-17 — Request body limits.** A body over 5000000 bytes answers `413` `payload_too_large` and a body that is not valid JSON answers `400` `validation`, each storing nothing.
- **DP-18 — Segment input bounds.** A segment name outside three to sixty characters or an index outside the activity's points answers `400`, and another athlete's activity answers `403` or `404`.
- **DP-19 — Segment creation.** A segment made from two points of the caller's own activity carries their rounded distance and elevation difference and matches existing activities at creation, and its creator may rename it with the new name shown everywhere.
- **DP-20 — Segment matching.** An activity gains an effort when it passes within 25 metres of the start and later within 25 metres of the end, timed between those points, with at most one effort per activity on each segment.
- **DP-21 — Leaderboard ranking.** A leaderboard holds each athlete's fastest effort sorted by time with ties to the earlier start, ranks again when an athlete deletes their account, and needs a session.
- **DP-22 — Personal records and crowns.** Each effort keeps the `pr_rank` and `is_crown` it earned when recorded, and an `only_me` effort earns a personal record but never a crown.
- **DP-23 — Seeded Harbor Climb.** The seeded Harbor Climb segment has its seeded distance, elevation difference and leaderboard, and its page crowns Leo Park in first place.
- **DP-24 — Segment page.** The segment page meets the accessibility, touch target, font and reflow rules.
- **DP-25 — Leaderboard depth by plan.** A non-subscriber receives the first ten entries plus their own `viewer_entry`, and a subscriber receives every entry.
- **DP-26 — Leaderboard visibility.** Efforts from `only_me` activities never appear on a leaderboard, while efforts from `followers` activities appear to every viewer.
- **DP-27 — Following leaderboard filter.** `filter=following` keeps the viewer and the athletes they follow, ranked again from 1, and the segment page's Following tab lives in the address.
- **DP-28 — Plans and trial.** `GET /api/plans` lists the four plans, choosing a plan starts a `trialing` subscription that ends thirty days later and makes the athlete a subscriber, monthly billing exists only for `individual`, and cancelling ends the subscription.
- **DP-29 — Subscription page.** The subscription page switches the Individual price between annual and monthly, shows the Family and Student prices and loops its pulse ring every three seconds, and meets the accessibility, touch target, font and reduced-motion rules.
- **DP-30 — Follows.** Following a public athlete is accepted at once with counts including accepted follows only, following yourself answers `400`, an unknown athlete answers `404`, and unfollowing answers `204`.
- **DP-31 — Feed and paging.** The feed lists the caller's own and followed activities newest first with ties by id descending, pages each activity exactly once through an opaque cursor even as new activities arrive, and refuses a `limit` outside 1 to 50.
- **DP-32 — No email or password leaks.** No response carries another athlete's email address, a password or a password hash.
- **DP-33 — Dashboard feed.** The dashboard shows a followed athlete's new activity in a feed whose cards rise in, and meets the accessibility, touch target and font rules.
- **DP-34 — Private follow requests.** Following a private athlete waits `pending` with that athlete's activities answered as not found, only the followed athlete may approve or reject, approval grants access, and rejection removes the request.
- **DP-35 — Seeded follows.** Ava follows Leo, Leo and Sam follow Ava, and Mia's account is private.
- **DP-36 — Athlete profile.** The profile's Follow button reads Requested or Following after a press, and profile and dashboard pages meet the accessibility, touch target, font and reflow rules.
- **DP-37 — Deletion removes follows.** Deleting an account removes its follows in both directions and its pending requests, and its profile then answers `404`.
- **DP-38 — Activity visibility.** A hidden activity answers `404` on every activity route and never appears in another athlete's feed or list, and the follower view of an activity page meets the accessibility rules.
- **DP-39 — Owner-only changes.** Another athlete's patch, delete or export of a visible activity answers `403` and changes nothing.
- **DP-40 — Privacy zones.** Streams returned to other athletes omit every point within the privacy zone radius while the owner keeps every point and the figures never change, and a radius other than 200, 400, 800 or 1600 answers `400`.
- **DP-41 — Privacy settings page.** The privacy settings form saves a privacy zone and confirms with Privacy settings saved, and the page meets the accessibility, touch target and font rules.
- **DP-42 — Unknown ids.** An id in a route that names no record answers `404` whatever its form.
- **DP-43 — Kudos once per athlete.** Kudos count once per athlete under repeats and simultaneous requests, ten simultaneous kudos from different athletes count ten, the stored data holds one kudos row per athlete and activity, and kudos need a session.
- **DP-44 — Kudos rules and list.** Kudos on your own activity answers `422` `cannot_kudo_own_activity`, and the kudos list keeps the order the kudos were given.
- **DP-45 — Kudos toggle.** The kudos toggle raises the count at once with the kudos pop and returns to its previous state with the Kudos could not be saved alert when refused, and the activity page meets the focus, accessibility and font rules.
- **DP-46 — Stored collections.** The stored data uses the collection names listed in Business rules.
- **DP-47 — Comments.** Comments of one to five hundred characters after trimming are stored and listed oldest first, other text answers `400` naming `text`, `comment_count` always matches the list, and comments need a session.
- **DP-48 — Comment deletion rule.** Only the comment's author or the activity owner may delete a comment, and anyone else receives `403`.
- **DP-49 — Comment thread.** The comment form posts a comment, a refused comment never stays in the thread and keeps its text in the field with the Comment could not be posted alert, and the page meets the accessibility, touch target and font rules.
- **DP-50 — Dashboard paging.** Reaching the end of the dashboard feed loads the next page, and the dashboard stays still under reduced motion and never scrolls sideways.
- **DP-51 — Deletion removes kudos and comments.** Deleting an account removes the kudos it gave and the comments it wrote from every count and list.
- **DP-52 — Monthly challenges.** The eight seeded challenges have their dimensions and goals and run over the current calendar month in UTC or in the caller's time zone.
- **DP-53 — Challenge progress.** Progress is zero until joined and then sums the caller's qualifying activities of any visibility this month, joining again answers `200`, and leaving answers `204`.
- **DP-54 — Challenge completion.** A joined challenge completes at the qualifying activity in start-time order that reaches the goal, deleting an activity recomputes it, and completed challenges appear in the trophies.
- **DP-55 — Challenges page.** Each joined challenge shows a progress ring with its whole percentage capped at 100 and Completed when done, and the page meets the accessibility, touch target, font and reflow rules.
- **DP-56 — Weekly stats.** Weekly stats hold zero-filled Monday weeks in the athlete's time zone, twelve by default with the year to date, and refuse `weeks` outside 1 to 52.
- **DP-57 — Year recap.** The recap totals the caller's activities in the year with the longest activity, the top sport by count with ties going to distance, and the kudos received.
- **DP-58 — Recap player.** Play recap plays the music and advances a card every five seconds, Pause recap stops both, Mute, Next and Previous work, nothing advances under reduced motion, and the page meets the focus, accessibility, font and reflow rules.
- **DP-59 — Owner edits and history.** The owner's `PATCH` updates the name and visibility, and the history lists each field a patch actually changed, oldest first.
- **DP-60 — GPX export.** The export is a GPX 1.1 document holding every point, and uploading it reproduces the same figures.
- **DP-61 — Activity deletion.** Deleting an activity removes it with its efforts, kudos and comments and stops it counting toward leaderboards and stats.
- **DP-62 — Elevation profile and flyover.** The Route position slider steps through the points with its value text, and Play flyover moves a marker along the route reporting its running distance, with speeds of 1x, 2x and 4x.
- **DP-63 — Owner controls.** The owner sees Edit, Delete, Export GPX and Create segment, Play audio splits is disabled without speech synthesis, and the activity page stays still under reduced motion and meets the accessibility and font rules on a phone.
- **DP-64 — Home page.** The home page shows the headline, the Sign Up With Email action, the gravel road photograph with alternative text and the muted looping hero clip, and its Google and Apple buttons explain that only email sign up is available.
- **DP-65 — Hero captions.** The hero clip carries an English captions track whose `/media/hero.vtt` file is served as `text/vtt` WebVTT holding the single cue.
- **DP-66 — Licensed media.** Each media file the app ships is free to use and served from the app's own origin at `/assets/<file name>` with its media type.
- **DP-67 — Reduced motion on the home page.** Under a reduced-motion preference the hero clip stays paused at its first frame until Play video is pressed, and nothing animates on the home page.
- **DP-68 — Home motion.** The hero route line draws itself on load, product images rise in as they enter view, and the header hides on scroll down and returns on scroll up.
- **DP-69 — Consent bar.** The consent bar remembers being dismissed.
- **DP-70 — Home accessibility.** The home, features and What's New pages meet the touch target, focus, accessibility, font and reflow rules.
- **DP-71 — Challenge filter chips.** The chosen challenge filter chip lives in the address as `?filter=`, narrows the galleries and is restored by Back.
- **DP-72 — Public pages accessibility.** The public pages and the maps page meet WCAG 2.2 AA, reflow at 320 pixels, and meet the touch target and font rules.
- **DP-73 — Navigation drawer.** On a phone the Open menu button opens a drawer that keeps focus inside it and closes on Escape, returning focus to the button.
- **DP-74 — Feature tabs.** The TRAIN, EXPLORE and COMPETE tabs switch panels by click and by arrow keys.
- **DP-75 — Team price calculator.** The team price calculator shows the price per team member and the total price per year for each tier.
- **DP-76 — Maps explorer.** The maps search narrows segments by name and opens a side panel with distance and grade, and Create Route explains that route building is coming soon.
- **DP-77 — Health.** `GET /api/health` answers `200` at the public URL once the app is ready.
- **DP-78 — Not found.** An address that does not exist answers `404` with a not-found page carrying a link home.
- **DP-79 — Gift page.** The gift page shows the login page with a note about gifting.
- **DP-80 — API errors.** Every `/api` error answers JSON with an `error` string and never a stack trace, SQL text, file path or library name.
- **DP-81 — Colour and heroes.** One orange marks every primary action and route line on white cards, and the paid-feature heroes glow from black through maroon into ember.
- **DP-82 — Shape, type and marks.** Corners, pills, shadows, the logo, the sport glyphs and the one grotesque sans family follow Visual design.
- **DP-83 — Marketing pages.** The family, student, about and What's New pages carry the plan cards, feature tables, question accordions, team captains and story cards this brief lists.
- **DP-84 — Responsive layout.** Feature grids sit two across at tablet width and challenge cards four across on a wide desktop, and on a phone the signup card leads and wide tables scroll in their own frame.
- **DP-85 — Engineering standards.** Figures come only from the parsed points, and the parsing, visibility and uniqueness rules each live in one place in the source.

## Backend

### API

The application serves a JSON interface under the `/api` prefix on the same origin as its pages. The health route and the authentication routes need no session; every other `/api` route needs one and answers `401` without it. The session is a bearer token in the `Authorization` header, returned as `token` by sign up and log in. Times are ISO 8601 in UTC with a trailing `Z`, such as `2026-10-05T07:00:00Z`. Invalid input answers `400` with `{ "error": "validation", "fields": { ... } }` naming each failing field, and stores nothing.

**Errors and limits.** Every `/api` error answers JSON carrying an `error` string, never an HTML page, a stack trace, SQL text, a file path or a library name. An id in a route that names no existing record answers `404`, whatever its form, so `abc`, `-1`, `1.5` and `99999999999999999999` are each answered `404`. A request body that is not valid JSON answers `400` with `{ "error": "validation" }` and stores nothing. A request body larger than 5000000 bytes answers `413` with `{ "error": "payload_too_large" }` and stores nothing, while a body up to that size, such as a GPX document of 4500000 bytes, is accepted as normal.

**Health.** `GET /api/health` returns `200` once the app is ready.

**Sessions.** `POST /api/auth/signup` with `{ email, name, password }` returns `201` with `{ token, athlete }`; the email must be a valid address, the name one to eighty characters and the password at least eight characters, otherwise `400`; an email already registered answers `409` with `{ "error": "email_taken" }`. `POST /api/auth/login` with `{ email, password }` returns `200` with `{ token, athlete }` or `401`. `POST /api/auth/logout` returns `204` and that token no longer works. `GET /api/me` returns `200` with `{ athlete }`. An athlete carries `id`, `email`, `name`, `private`, `default_visibility`, `subscriber`, `plan` and `time_zone`. This full record, with its `email`, goes only to the athlete themselves; every other response describes another athlete only through the fields listed for it, such as `athlete_id` and `athlete_name`, `follower_id` and `follower_name`, or `{ id, name }`.

**Uploading.** `POST /api/activities` takes `{ name, sport_type, visibility, gpx }`, where `gpx` is the text of a GPX 1.1 document whose `trkpt` elements carry `lat`, `lon`, an `ele` child and a `time` child. `name` is one to one hundred characters, `sport_type` is `run`, `ride`, `walk` or `hike`, and `visibility` is `everyone`, `followers` or `only_me`, defaulting to the athlete's `default_visibility`. A document that does not parse, or has fewer than two track points with a time, answers `400` naming `gpx`. A valid upload returns `201` with the activity. An activity carries `id`, `athlete_id`, `athlete_name`, `name`, `sport_type`, `visibility`, `start_time` (the first point's time), `distance_m`, `moving_time_s`, `elapsed_time_s`, `elevation_gain_m`, `avg_pace_s_per_km`, `avg_speed_kmh`, `kudos_count`, `comment_count`, `splits`, `best_efforts` and `segment_efforts`.

**Duplicates and replays.** A second upload by the same athlete whose first point has the same time as one of their existing activities answers `409` with `{ "error": "duplicate_activity", "activity_id": ... }` naming the existing activity, and stores nothing. `POST /api/activities` accepts an `Idempotency-Key` request header scoped to the athlete: a replay with the same key and the same body answers `200` or `201` with the same activity `id` as the first answer and must not create a second activity. Reusing a key with a different body answers `422` with `{ "error": "idempotency_key_reused" }`. Three identical uploads sent at the same moment by one athlete resolve to exactly one `201`; the others answer `409`, and exactly one activity is stored. The stored answer for a key is kept with the activity it created. Choose any mechanism.

**Reading and changing activities.** `GET /api/activities/{id}` returns the activity. `GET /api/activities/{id}/streams` returns `{ latlng, altitude, distance, time }`, four arrays of equal length with one entry per point: `[lat, lon]` pairs, `ele` values, running distance in whole metres and seconds since the first point. `PATCH /api/activities/{id}` with any of `{ name, visibility }` returns `200` with the updated activity. `DELETE /api/activities/{id}` returns `204`; the activity, its efforts, kudos and comments are gone and it no longer counts toward leaderboards, challenges or stats. `GET /api/activities/{id}/export.gpx` returns `200` with content type `application/gpx+xml` and a GPX 1.1 document holding every point with its `lat`, `lon`, `ele` and `time`, so uploading the export reproduces the same figures. Only the owner may patch, delete or export: another athlete receives `403`, or `404` when the activity is hidden from them. `GET /api/activities/{id}/history` answers the owner with `{ changes }`, oldest first, with edits made at the same instant kept in the order they were made, holding one `{ field, from, to, changed_at }` entry for each field whose value a `PATCH` actually changed, so a `PATCH` that repeats the current value adds nothing; anyone else receives `403`, or `404` when the activity is hidden from them. A renamed activity shows its new name at once on every surface that lists it. `GET /api/athletes/{id}/activities` lists that athlete's activities the caller can see, newest first, with the same `limit=` and `next_cursor` paging as the feed.

**Privacy settings.** `GET /api/me/privacy` and `PUT /api/me/privacy` read and write `{ private, default_visibility, privacy_zone }`, where `privacy_zone` is `null` or `{ lat, lon, radius_m }` with `radius_m` one of `200`, `400`, `800` or `1600`; any other radius answers `400` naming `privacy_zone`.

**Account settings.** `PATCH /api/me` with any of `{ name, time_zone }` returns `200` with `{ athlete }`. `time_zone` is an IANA zone name such as `Asia/Kolkata` or `America/Los_Angeles` and is `UTC` for a new athlete; a name that is not an IANA zone answers `400` naming `time_zone`. `DELETE /api/me` with `{ password }` returns `204` when the password is the account's; a wrong or missing password answers `403` with `{ "error": "wrong_password" }` and changes nothing.

**Follows.** `POST /api/athletes/{id}/follow` returns `201` with `{ "status": "accepted" }` for a public athlete or `{ "status": "pending" }` for a private one; repeating it returns `200` with the same status, following yourself answers `400` and an unknown athlete `404`. `DELETE /api/athletes/{id}/follow` returns `204`. `GET /api/follow-requests` lists `{ requests }` pending for the caller, each with `id`, `follower_id` and `follower_name`; `POST /api/follow-requests/{id}/approve` returns `200` with `{ "status": "accepted" }` and `POST /api/follow-requests/{id}/reject` returns `200` with `{ "status": "rejected" }` and removes the request. Only the athlete being followed may approve or reject; anyone else receives `403` or `404`. `GET /api/athletes/{id}` returns `{ id, name, private, follower_count, following_count, follow_status }`, where `follow_status` is `none`, `pending`, `accepted` or `self` from the caller's side, and counts include accepted follows only.

**Feed and paging.** `GET /api/feed` takes `limit=` from 1 to 50, defaulting to 20, and an opaque cursor, and answers `{ "activities": [...], "next_cursor": ... }`: the caller's own activities and the activities of athletes they follow with an accepted follow, excluding anything hidden from the caller, ordered by `start_time` newest first with ties broken by `id` descending. At most `limit` activities come back; passing `next_cursor` back as `cursor` returns the following page, and `next_cursor` is `null` on the last page, so walking every page returns each activity exactly once. Every later page is fetched with the opaque cursor the previous page returned, so new activities arriving between requests never repeat or skip an activity already walked. A `limit` outside 1 to 50 answers `400`.

**Kudos and comments.** `POST /api/activities/{id}/kudos` returns `201` with `{ kudos_count }` the first time and `200` with the unchanged count on a repeat; kudos on your own activity answers `422` with `{ "error": "cannot_kudo_own_activity" }`. `DELETE /api/activities/{id}/kudos` returns `204`. `GET /api/activities/{id}/kudos` returns `{ athletes }`, each with `id` and `name`, in the order the kudos were given. `POST /api/activities/{id}/comments` with `{ text }` of one to five hundred characters after trimming returns `201` with `{ id, athlete_id, athlete_name, text, created_at }`; `GET /api/activities/{id}/comments` returns `{ comments }` oldest first; `DELETE /api/comments/{id}` returns `204` for the comment's author or the activity's owner and `403` for anyone else. `kudos_count` and `comment_count` on the activity always match these lists.

**Segments.** `POST /api/segments` with `{ activity_id, start_index, end_index, name }` creates a segment from two points of the caller's own activity and returns `201` with `{ id, name, start_lat, start_lon, end_lat, end_lon, distance_m, elevation_diff_m }`. The indexes must satisfy `0 <= start_index < end_index <` the number of points and the name must be three to sixty characters, otherwise `400`; another athlete's activity answers `403` or `404`. `GET /api/segments/{id}` returns the segment with `effort_count` and `athlete_count`, and `GET /api/segments?q=<text>` answers `{ segments }`, the segments whose name contains the text, ignoring letter case. `PATCH /api/segments/{id}` with `{ name }`, three to sixty characters, lets the segment's creator rename it and returns `200` with the segment; anyone else receives `403`. The new name shows at once wherever the segment appears: the segment itself, search and every activity's `segment_efforts`.

**Leaderboards.** `GET /api/segments/{id}/leaderboard` answers `{ entries, entry_count, viewer_entry }`, each entry `{ rank, athlete_id, athlete_name, elapsed_time_s, activity_id, start_time }` with `rank` counting from 1. `entry_count` is the total number of entries. `viewer_entry` is the caller's own entry with its rank, or `null`. The leaderboard takes `filter=overall`, the default, or `filter=following`; any other `filter` answers `400` naming `filter`. Each activity's `segment_efforts` lists `{ segment_id, segment_name, elapsed_time_s, pr_rank, is_crown }`.

**Challenges.** `GET /api/challenges` returns `{ challenges }`, each `{ id, name, sport_types, dimension, goal, starts_at, ends_at, joined, progress, completed, completed_at }`. `POST /api/challenges/{id}/join` returns `201` and then `200` on repeat; `DELETE /api/challenges/{id}/join` returns `204`. `GET /api/athletes/{id}/trophies` lists the completed joined challenges.

**Stats and recap.** `GET /api/athletes/me/stats` takes `weeks` from 1 to 52, defaulting to 12, and answers `{ weeks, year_to_date }`: exactly that many entries `{ week_start, distance_m, moving_time_s, elevation_gain_m, activity_count }`, oldest first. Any other `weeks` answers `400`. `GET /api/athletes/me/recap?year=YYYY` answers `{ year, activity_count, distance_m, moving_time_s, elevation_gain_m, longest_activity_id, top_sport, kudos_received }`.

**Plans.** `GET /api/plans` returns `{ plans }`, each `{ id, name, annual_price_cents, monthly_price_cents, seats }`: `free` (Free, 0, 0, 1 seat), `individual` (Individual, 7999, 1199, 1 seat), `family` (Family, 12999, `null`, 4 seats) and `student` (Student, 3999, `null`, 1 seat). `POST /api/subscription` with `{ plan, billing }`, `billing` being `annual` or `monthly`, returns `201` with `{ plan, billing, status, trial_ends_on }` where `status` is `trialing` (the only status in scope) and `trial_ends_on` is the UTC date thirty days after today; the athlete's `subscriber` becomes `true` and `plan` names the plan. Monthly billing exists only for `individual`; any other combination or plan answers `400`. `DELETE /api/subscription` returns `204` and the athlete is no longer a subscriber.

### Business rules

**Parsing rules.** The parsing rules are exact, so two correct builds agree. Take the points in file order. The distance between consecutive points is the haversine great-circle distance on a sphere of radius 6371000 metres: with latitudes and longitudes in radians, `d = 2 * 6371000 * asin(sqrt(sin^2((lat2 - lat1) / 2) + cos(lat1) * cos(lat2) * sin^2((lon2 - lon1) / 2)))`. `distance_m` is the sum of those distances; `elapsed_time_s` is the last time minus the first time in seconds; `moving_time_s` sums the time of every consecutive pair whose time gap is positive and whose distance divided by that gap is at least 0.5 metres per second; `elevation_gain_m` sums every rise in `ele` from one point to the next and ignores falls. `avg_pace_s_per_km` is `moving_time_s` divided by `distance_m` over one thousand, rounded to a whole number of seconds, for runs, walks and hikes, and is `null` for rides. `avg_speed_kmh` is filled for every sport, runs, walks and hikes included: `distance_m` divided by `moving_time_s` times 3.6, rounded to one decimal place (and `0` when `moving_time_s` is `0`); it is the only figure kept to a decimal. Every other figure is rounded to the nearest whole number, halves rounding up.

**Splits and best efforts.** `splits` lists one entry per whole kilometre and then the remainder. With `c` the running distance and `t` the time at each point, the time at which the running distance first reaches `k` kilometres is found by straight-line interpolation between the two points either side of that distance; split `k` covers `1000` metres and its `elapsed_time_s` is that crossing time minus the previous crossing time, the first split starting at the first point's time. When at least one metre remains after the last whole kilometre, a final split covers the remaining distance up to the last point. Each split is `{ split, distance_m, elapsed_time_s, pace_s_per_km }`, numbered from 1, where `pace_s_per_km` is `elapsed_time_s` divided by `distance_m` over one thousand. For runs, `best_efforts` lists `{ name, distance_m, elapsed_time_s }` for `400m` (400 metres), `1k` (1000 metres) and `5k` (5000 metres), each only when the activity is at least that long: the effort is the smallest time difference between two points, the later minus the earlier, whose running distances differ by at least that many metres. Rides, walks and hikes have empty `best_efforts`.

**Visibility.** The owner always sees their own activity. An `only_me` activity is hidden from everyone else. A `followers` activity is visible only to athletes whose follow of the owner is accepted. An `everyone` activity is visible to every signed-in athlete, except that a private athlete's activities are visible only to their accepted followers. A hidden activity answers `404` from every activity route, including streams, kudos, comments and export, and never appears in another athlete's feed, activity list or search; a segment leaderboard still lists its effort, as described under Leaderboard rules.

**Privacy zones.** When the owner has a privacy zone, every stream returned to any other athlete omits each point within `radius_m` of the zone centre by the haversine distance, from all four arrays together; the owner still receives every point, and the activity's figures never change.

**Time zones.** The athlete's time zone sets their calendar: weekly stats weeks, the year to date, the recap year and the monthly challenge window are all taken in that zone, and instants are still written in UTC.

**Account deletion.** Deleting an account erases the athlete at once: their sessions stop working, their email and password no longer log in, and the email may sign up again as a new athlete; `GET /api/athletes/{id}` for them answers `404`; their activities, with the points, efforts, kudos and comments on them, are gone; the kudos they gave and the comments they wrote are removed, so every `kudos_count`, `comment_count` and kudos or comment list drops them; their follows in both directions and their pending follow requests are removed, so every `follower_count` and `following_count` drops them; and their efforts leave every leaderboard, which ranks again without them with its `entry_count` reduced. Segments they created stay for everyone else.

**Kudos under load.** Ten different athletes giving kudos at the same moment leave `kudos_count` at exactly ten, and one athlete sending five kudos at the same moment gets exactly one `201` and moves the count by one.

**Segment geometry and matching.** A new segment's start is the point at `start_index` and its end the point at `end_index`; `distance_m` is the difference of their running distances and `elevation_diff_m` the end `ele` minus the start `ele`, both rounded. Creating a segment matches every existing activity against it, and every later upload is matched against every segment. An activity matches a segment when some point lies within 25 metres of the segment start, and a later point lies within 25 metres of the segment end: the effort starts at the first point within 25 metres of the start, ends at the first later point within 25 metres of the end, and its `elapsed_time_s` is the difference of their times. An activity has at most one effort per segment. When a new segment matches existing activities, they are matched in `start_time` order.

**Leaderboard rules.** A leaderboard holds one entry per athlete, their fastest effort, sorted by `elapsed_time_s` with ties going to the earlier effort start time. Efforts from `only_me` activities never appear and are not counted in `entry_count`; every other effort appears in full entry shape even when its activity is hidden from the caller. An `only_me` effort never earns a crown but still counts toward its athlete's own `pr_rank`. A subscriber receives every entry; any other athlete receives only the first ten. `filter=following` keeps only the caller's own entry and the entries of athletes the caller follows with an accepted follow, ranked again from 1 within that set, with `entry_count` and `viewer_entry` counted within it and the same ten-row limit for non-subscribers. `pr_rank` is `1`, `2` or `3` when, at the moment the effort was recorded, it was the athlete's fastest, second or third fastest effort on that segment, and `null` otherwise; `is_crown` is `true` when, at that moment, it ranked first on the leaderboard.

**Challenge rules.** Every challenge runs over the current calendar month in UTC, or in the caller's time zone when they chose another: `starts_at` is the first instant of the month and `ends_at` the first instant of the next month in that zone, both written in UTC, so for an athlete in `Asia/Kolkata` November begins at `2026-10-31T18:30:00Z`. An activity qualifies when it is the caller's own, of any visibility, its sport is in `sport_types` and its `start_time` falls in the window. `progress` is the sum of `distance_m` for `distance`, of `elevation_gain_m` for `elevation`, of `moving_time_s` for `moving_time`, and the largest single `distance_m` for `single_distance`; it counts qualifying activities whether they were uploaded before or after joining, and it is `0` while the caller has not joined. `completed` is `true` when a joined challenge's progress reaches `goal`, and `completed_at` is then the `start_time` of the qualifying activity that brought it to the goal, taking activities in `start_time` order, and otherwise `null`. Deleting an activity recomputes both.

**Stats rules.** Each week begins on Monday at 00:00 UTC, or at 00:00 in the athlete's time zone when they chose another; `week_start` is that Monday as `YYYY-MM-DD` in that zone, the last entry is the current week, activities are counted by `start_time`, and weeks with no activity carry zeros. `year_to_date` holds `{ distance_m, activity_count }` for the current UTC year, or the current year in the athlete's time zone.

**Recap rules.** The recap covers the caller's activities that start in that year, taken in the caller's time zone (`UTC` unless they chose another), as for the year to date: `longest_activity_id` has the greatest `distance_m`, `top_sport` is the sport with the most activities, ties going to the greater total distance, and `kudos_received` totals the kudos on those activities. A year with no activities answers zeros and `null`.

**Plans.** Choosing a plan starts a 30-day trial, takes no payment and makes the athlete a subscriber.

**Stored data.** The stored records live in these collections, using exactly these names: `athletes`, `sessions`, `follows`, `activities`, `activity_points`, `segments`, `segment_efforts`, `kudos`, `comments`, `challenges`, `challenge_participants`, `privacy_zones`, `subscriptions` and `activity_edits`. An athlete has an id, a unique email, a name, a protected password, a private flag, a default visibility, a plan, a time zone and a created time. A follow links a follower to a followee with a status of `pending` or `accepted`. An activity belongs to an athlete and has a name, a sport, a visibility, a start time, its computed figures, its splits and best efforts, and a created time; its points are kept in order with latitude, longitude, elevation and time. A segment has a name, start and end coordinates, a distance, an elevation difference and its creator. A segment effort links a segment, an activity and an athlete with its elapsed time, start time, personal-record rank and crown flag. A kudos links an athlete and an activity, at most once. A comment belongs to an activity and an author and has text and a created time. A challenge has a name, sport types, a dimension, a goal and a monthly window; a participant links an athlete to a challenge with a joined time. A privacy zone has a centre and a radius. A subscription has an athlete, a plan, a billing period, a status and a trial end date. An activity edit records the activity, the field, the old and new values and the time of the change.

**Uniqueness.** These uniqueness rules must hold in the stored data itself under simultaneous requests, so an application-level check alone does not satisfy them: one activity per athlete and first-point time, one kudos per athlete and activity, one effort per activity and segment, and one stored answer per athlete and `Idempotency-Key`. Times are stored in UTC.

## Technical requirements

### Deployment

Python 3 (the image's `python3`) and Node.js 22 with npm are available; the backend is written in Python. Deliver the source under `/app`. The app is installed and run as an unprivileged user. Python dependencies go into a `.venv` inside `/app`, and the manifest calls tools by path (`.venv/bin/<tool>`) or as `python -m <tool>`.

`/app/run.json` holds the string commands `install`, `build` and `serve`, plus the schema marker. For example, with your own entry point in place of the module name:

```json
{"_schema":"run-manifest-v1","install":"python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt","build":".venv/bin/python -m compileall -q .","serve":".venv/bin/python -m server --host 0.0.0.0 --port ${APP_PUBLIC_PORT:-4173}"}
```

- `serve` runs a production server in the foreground behind a static or preview server for the built pages, never a dev server or an auto-reloader.
- It binds `0.0.0.0` on the port in `APP_PUBLIC_PORT`, which defaults to 4173, and the app is reached at `APP_PUBLIC_URL`. Read both from the environment; never hardcode either.
- `GET /api/health` returns `200` once the app is ready.
- Grading copies `/app` and runs install, build and serve with freshly provisioned database credentials on an empty database. The app starts with no manual steps.

## Engineering standards

- **Domain language.** The code is idiomatic Python. Its names use this brief's domain terms: athlete, activity, track point, split, best effort, segment, effort, leaderboard, personal record, crown, kudos, follow request, privacy zone, challenge, recap and plan.
- **One home per rule.** The haversine distance, the parsing rules, the visibility rule and the leaderboard ranking are each decided in exactly one place in the source.
- **Server-side figures.** Every stored figure is computed on the server from the parsed track points, never taken from the client.
- **Database guarantees.** The uniqueness rules are enforced by unique constraints in the database, and simultaneous outcomes are settled by the database, never by an earlier read checked later.
- **Errors.** Every API error comes from one shared error shape.
- **No excess code.** There is no dead, commented-out or unreachable code, no defensive check for an impossible state, no abstraction with a single implementation, and no feature, route or setting beyond this brief.
