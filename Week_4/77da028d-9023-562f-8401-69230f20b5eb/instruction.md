# Umbral Works Darkframe

Darkframe is a personal workspace for one backyard deep-sky imager. It holds a plan for tonight checked against the imager's own painted treeline, the Moon and the sky conditions they enter by hand. It also holds a log of each session, a ledger of how many hours each filter still needs, and a calibration library that says which dark, flat and bias frames may calibrate which lights. It runs as four cooperating Python services behind one public port. It stores records in PostgreSQL, frame files and calibration masters in MinIO, and sends go/no-go, clear-window and new-moon reminder pushes to the imager's phone through ntfy. Out of scope: user accounts beyond the workspace key described below, any forecast or weather provider (the imager types conditions in), stacking or image processing, thumbnails generated by the server, social features beyond one read-only project link, payments, and any datastore other than those three services.

## Problem and users

A backyard deep-sky imager gets perhaps forty usable nights a year and spreads them across several projects that take many nights each, for example a nebula in hydrogen-alpha (Ha), oxygen-III (OIII) and sulphur-II (SII) over a dozen sessions. Today the plan lives in a sky-chart app, the session notes in a spreadsheet or forum thread, and the calibration frames in a folder tree. The same mistakes keep costing them nights:

- They shoot a target that is behind the neighbour's oak or too close to the Moon.
- They lose track of how many hours per filter are still owed to finish a project.
- They stack with darks whose gain, offset, sensor temperature or exposure differ from the lights, which gives a black or noisy result.
- They keep old darks past their useful life.

Altitude planners and logbooks exist, but none of them ties the plan, the night's log and the calibration library together.

Users:

- **Imager (weekly, at home).** Plans tonight's targets against their own treeline, the Moon and the filters still owed. Logs the session afterwards, sees hours owed per filter, and keeps a matched, fresh calibration library.
- **Imager away from home (occasional, on a phone).** Checks the log and tonight's go/no-go at the telescope or on the road, receives a push when the night turns go, and sends a friend a read-only project page.
- **Friend (no workspace).** Opens a shared link and reads a project's integration summary. They never see the imager's notes or exact home position.

## Journeys

1. **Set up the site.** Start: no workspace. The imager creates a workspace with one API request (no screen creates workspaces), keeps the workspace key, unlocks with it on `/unlock`, and edits the site label, latitude, longitude, elevation and time zone on the Site screen. On the Site screen they paint the treeline by dragging across the horizon painter, or with the arrow keys. End: after a reload the painter shows the same treeline, and the verdict card on the Site screen reflects it.
2. **Plan tonight.** Start: the imager is unlocked with active projects and no plan for tonight. They open Plan, see the no-conditions banner and an unknown verdict, add two targets, open the sky conditions dialog and enter cloud, transparency and seeing for each hour. End: the verdict card shows go or no-go, the best target and filter, the clear window and the countdown to it, all matching what the server reports.
3. **Scrub the night.** Start: tonight's plan with targets and conditions. The imager moves the night time handle from dusk to dawn with the keyboard, then drags it. A target drifts behind the painted treeline in both the flat sky chart and the 3D dome. The lens over the chart reveals the hidden sky and reads out the target's altitude and Moon distance. End: the usable minutes, verdict and hours owed after tonight have counted down in place, and the last announcement names the handle's time.
4. **At the telescope on a phone.** Start: a phone in portrait with reduced motion turned on. The imager checks the verdict card, steps the night with the bottom bar's buttons using one thumb, and orbits the dome with a finger. Each change shows its end state at once. When ntfy is unreachable, the Site screen's push log says the push is not sent yet. End: once ntfy answers, the phone receives that push once.
5. **Log a session.** Start: an active project. The imager creates a session, starts imaging at 23:30 site time, adds sub-exposure batches (a repeated submit is ignored), uploads frames one by one while progress is announced, links a matching dark set (a mismatched one is refused with the reason), and finishes the session. End: the session sits on the evening's date, its frames are counted, and the project's hours owed have dropped by exactly the logged integration.
6. **Amend, trash and restore.** Start: a logged session. The imager opens the session editor and types notes. The edit lock runs out and the notes stay. They amend a batch's sub count, trash the session, then restore it. End: the session is identical to its state before the trash, and hours owed match the amended batches.
7. **Keep the library.** Start: an empty library showing the empty-library copy. The imager adds a dark set, uploads its master file, marks it ready, edits its temperature, then restores the earlier version. Old sets show the stale warning, and the imager retires one. End: the library table shows each set's state and age, and the restored set has its original fields and master file.
8. **Share and be reminded.** Start: an active project and the new-moon weekend reminder turned on. The imager creates a share link and a friend opens it without a key, seeing the integration summary with only rounded site coordinates. The imager regenerates the link and the old link dies. The upcoming reminders list keeps the chosen evening time across the autumn clock change. End: after the link is revoked, the friend sees the unavailable copy.

## Mandatory requirements

This is the complete list of mandatory requirements; every check traces to one of these lines and the detailed sections below.

- **DP-01 — Workspace creation and unlock** Creating a workspace returns a one-time workspace key, and every private API call and screen requires that key as a bearer token or the unlock cookie, as specified in API.
- **DP-02 — Owner-only records** Every project, session, batch, frame, calibration set, plan, push and share setting is visible and changeable only by its imager, and another imager's id anywhere in a path or body answers 404.
- **DP-03 — Site and horizon settings** The imager stores the site label, coordinates, elevation, time zone, minimum altitude, push topic and a 72-bin horizon mask, with the validation stated in API.
- **DP-04 — Painting the treeline** The Site screen's horizon painter changes mask bins by pointer drag and by keyboard, saves without a submit button, and the tonight verdict card on the same screen updates from the server's reply.
- **DP-05 — Site setup journey** A new imager creates a workspace, unlocks it, sets the site, paints the treeline and sees the saved mask and verdict card reflect it after reload.
- **DP-06 — Night window and ephemeris** The server computes the astronomical-night window, Sun, Moon and target positions, Moon illumination and separations by the published method and rounding, for any explicit observing date and minute.
- **DP-07 — Plan targets** The imager adds active projects' targets to a night's plan and removes them, with the conflicts stated in API.
- **DP-08 — Usable minutes and verdict** For each plan target and filter the server counts usable minutes from the chosen minute to the end of the filter's window, as qualified in Business rules, and derives the go, no-go or unknown verdict, best target, clear window and hours owed after tonight.
- **DP-09 — Server-decided tonight** The Plan screen without an explicit date shows the server's current observing night, minute, verdict and countdown even when the device clock is wrong.
- **DP-10 — No-conditions banner** A night with no sky conditions entered shows the published no-conditions banner and an unknown verdict.
- **DP-11 — Tonight plan journey** The imager opens tonight, adds two targets, enters conditions and sees the verdict, best target and clear window that the API reports.
- **DP-12 — Night time handle** The night time slider moves by published pointer and keyboard steps across the astronomical night and reports its minute and site time accessibly.
- **DP-13 — Figures recompute in place** Moving the handle, changing minimum altitude or adding or removing a target updates usable minutes, verdict, hours owed after tonight and total integration in place from the server, with one announcement per action.
- **DP-14 — Sky chart and treeline lens** The sky chart draws the painted treeline, target arcs and dots that dim behind the treeline, and a 160-pixel lens that reveals the unmasked sky and reads out the nearest target's altitude and Moon separation.
- **DP-15 — Three-dimensional sky dome** A WebGL dome shows the treeline band, target tracks, a lit Moon and a sidereal star field, stays still when untouched, orbits and zooms within published limits, follows the handle, and exposes its camera and minute in its accessible name.
- **DP-16 — Dome without WebGL** Without WebGL the dome is replaced by a still sky chart image with the same accessible name pattern and the published unavailable sentence, and every figure stays identical.
- **DP-17 — Reduced-motion scrub** Under reduced motion the handle, dome and numerals jump to their end state in one frame and the lens becomes a click-to-pin readout, with identical values.
- **DP-18 — One-handed phone plan** At 375 pixels wide the Plan screen keeps the time handle and step buttons in a bar at the bottom of the screen with touch targets of at least 44 pixels, and touch drags scrub and orbit.
- **DP-19 — Night scrub journey** The imager scrubs the night by keyboard from dusk to dawn and sees a target pass behind the treeline in both the chart and the dome while the verdict counts down.
- **DP-20 — Hourly sky conditions** The imager enters cloud, transparency and seeing per site-local hour of the night, and the server stores them with the time they were entered.
- **DP-21 — Stale conditions** Conditions older than the configured stale period are flagged stale by the server and shown with the published stale banner, as qualified in Business rules.
- **DP-22 — Clear-window countdown** Tonight's clear-window countdown comes from the server, refreshes at least every 60 seconds, and a refresh never closes an open dialog or replaces typed values or the latest announcement.
- **DP-23 — Sky conditions journey** The imager fills the conditions dialog, saves, watches the verdict change from unknown, and later sees the stale banner without losing values typed into a reopened dialog.
- **DP-24 — Session states** Sessions move planned, imaging, logged, amended, trashed only along the published edges, and every illegal edge answers 409.
- **DP-25 — Observing date across midnight** A session started before local noon belongs to the previous evening's observing date in the site's time zone, whatever the browser's or server's zone.
- **DP-26 — Idempotent batch submits** A batch submit repeated with the same key returns the stored response and creates nothing, and the same key with a different body answers 409.
- **DP-27 — Batch logging announcements** Adding a batch, logging and other session actions in the session editor are announced in the Session log region with the published copy.
- **DP-28 — Session logging journey** The imager creates a session, starts it at 23:30 site time, adds batches, logs it, and sees the evening's date and the project's owed hours drop by the batch integration.
- **DP-29 — Provider-confirmed frames** A frame upload is stored in object storage, confirmed, and only then counted, its bytes download exactly, and a storage failure counts nothing.
- **DP-30 — Upload progress announcements** Frame uploads announce progress, success with the session's frame count, and failure in the Upload progress region with the published copy.
- **DP-31 — Frame upload journey** The imager uploads two frames to a batch, sees each counted after it is stored, and downloads one back byte for byte.
- **DP-32 — Amending with reversals** Amending a logged session posts reversing entries for changed or removed batches and new entries for the amended ones, so owed seconds match the amended batches exactly.
- **DP-33 — Trash and exact restore** Trashing a session reverses its integration, and restoring it brings back its prior state, notes, batches, frames and calibration links unchanged and re-posts its integration.
- **DP-34 — Session editor lock** Opening the session editor takes an edit lock that expires after the configured period, and exactly one of two simultaneous lock requests wins.
- **DP-35 — Notes survive the lock timer** When the edit lock expires or another device holds it, the editor keeps the typed notes, shows the published copy, and saves them once the lock is taken again.
- **DP-36 — Amend and restore journey** The imager amends a batch count, trashes the session, restores it, and sees the same session contents and the project's hours owed return to the amended value.
- **DP-37 — Calibration sets and states** Calibration sets move building, ready, stale and retired along the published edges, with stale derived from the published age windows.
- **DP-38 — Master files** A calibration master upload is stored and confirmed before the set records it, and downloads byte for byte.
- **DP-39 — Calibration matching** The server links a calibration set to a light batch only when the published fields match within tolerance and refuses a mismatch with the published reasons.
- **DP-40 — Library copy** The empty library, the mismatch refusal and the stale warning render in the published words character for character.
- **DP-41 — Calibration versions** Every edit of a calibration set records a version, and restoring a version brings back its exact fields and master file.
- **DP-42 — Calibration library journey** The imager adds a dark set, uploads its master, marks it ready, links it to a matching batch, is refused for a mismatched one, edits it and restores the prior version.
- **DP-43 — Project states** Projects move draft, active, paused, complete, archived only along the published edges, with the published guards for activating and completing.
- **DP-44 — Paired hours-owed ledger** Every allotment change, logged batch, amendment, trash and restore posts movements of two entries that cancel, and planned, logged and owed seconds per filter equal sums of those entries.
- **DP-45 — Hours owed update in place** Changing a filter's planned time on the project page updates the hours owed table and total integration in place from the server and announces the new owed time.
- **DP-46 — Published breakpoints** Plan, Projects, Sessions and Library change layout exactly at 768 and 1200 pixels wide, as described in Screens and layout, and never scroll sideways at 375, 768 or 1280 pixels.
- **DP-47 — Project ledger journey** The imager activates a project, sets planned time per filter, logs a session against it, reads the ledger and marks the project complete once nothing is owed.
- **DP-48 — Revocable share links** The imager creates, regenerates, expires and revokes a read-only project link, the old link stops working at once, and the link can change nothing.
- **DP-49 — Shared page contents** The shared page and its API show only the published integration fields and site coordinates rounded to the half-degree grid, never notes or exact coordinates.
- **DP-50 — Sharing journey** The imager shares a project, a friend opens the link without unlocking and sees the summary, and after revocation the friend sees the unavailable copy.
- **DP-51 — Verdict pushes** Each change of a night's verdict to go or no-go queues one push that reaches the imager's ntfy topic with the published title and message, recorded as sent only after ntfy accepts it.
- **DP-52 — Push outage and recovery** While ntfy is unreachable the plan and log keep working, pushes show the published unsent state and retry, and each is delivered exactly once when ntfy answers again.
- **DP-53 — New-moon weekend reminder** The recurring reminder lists and sends occurrences at the imager's chosen site-local time before each new-moon weekend, keeping that wall-clock time across clock changes by the published rule.
- **DP-54 — Run manifest, services and health** One serve command starts the gateway and three internal services on published ports, the health endpoint reports each in the published shape, and every response carries a request id.
- **DP-55 — Server boundary** The server enforces the published size, content-type and same-origin limits, serves no source or credentials, and starts when a dependency is down.
- **DP-56 — Keyboard-complete dialogs and shortcuts** Every action works by keyboard, focus wraps inside every open dialog, Escape closes it and returns focus, and the published shortcuts work from any non-text control in their screen.
- **DP-57 — Motion timing** Small state changes take about a sixth of a second, sheets and dialogs take under half a second, nothing moves while idle, and reduced motion removes every transition.
- **DP-58 — Night-glow visual design** Every screen carries the dark ground, single cyan light, Sora type, card shapes and verdict card described in Visual design.
- **DP-59 — Treeline lens craft** The plan screen's dome, chart, lens and verdict read as one lit object on the dark ground, with the treeline, tracks and Moon drawn with the care described in Visual design.
- **DP-60 — Source integrity** Credentials come only from the environment, verdicts and figures are computed only on the server, the four services keep their own schemas, and three.js and Sora ship from the app's own files.

## Frontend

### Screens and layout (DP-04, DP-09, DP-14, DP-15, DP-18, DP-46)

Pages, all served by the gateway on the same origin:

- `/unlock`: the workspace key form, with the sky disc (no targets) as its hero. No screen creates a workspace; creation is the `POST /api/workspaces` request only. `/` sends a locked browser here and an unlocked one to `/plan`.
- `/plan`: tonight, as decided by the server (DP-09). `/plan?date=<date>` shows an explicit observing night. `<date>` is always `YYYY-MM-DD`.
- `/projects` and `/projects/<id>`: the project list and one project's page.
- `/sessions`: the session log. The session editor opens as a dialog over it.
- `/library`: the calibration library.
- `/site`: site settings, the horizon painter, the push log and the new-moon reminder.
- `/s/<token>`: the friend's read-only shared project page. It needs no key.

Every private screen except `/unlock` shows the **tonight verdict card**. It is the one bright card on the screen. On the Plan screen it is painted from the plan body for the screen's date at the handle's minute (the reply to `GET /api/plans/<date>?minute=<m>`, or to `GET /api/tonight` when the handle is at the server's current minute on tonight's date). On every other private screen it is painted from `GET /api/tonight`. Which plan fields fill its figures is defined in Business rules.

**Plan screen.** It contains:

- The headline `Night of <long date>`. `<long date>` is the weekday, day without a leading zero, month name and year, for example `Night of Tuesday 3 November 2026`.
- The verdict card.
- The 3D sky dome (DP-15).
- The flat sky chart with the lens (DP-14).
- The night time bar with its handle.
- The minimum altitude control.
- The plan target list.
- The single primary action, `Add target`.
- The `Enter sky conditions` button.
- The conditions notice region.

Layout by window width:

- **Below 768 pixels (checked at 375x812).** One column in this order: headline, verdict card, dome, sky chart, target list, `Enter sky conditions`, `Add target`. The time bar, its handle and the `Earlier 30 minutes` and `Later 30 minutes` buttons sit in a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen, within thumb reach (DP-18). Navigation is a tab bar at the bottom, above the time bar. The chart always sits above the target list.
- **768 to 1199 pixels (checked at 768x1024).** Navigation is a bar across the top. The headline and verdict card span the full width. Below them are two columns: the left one (about three fifths of the width) holds the dome, then the sky chart, then the time bar; the right one holds the target list and both buttons. The list's left edge is right of the chart's right edge.
- **1200 pixels and wider (checked at 1280x800).** Navigation is a rail down the left edge. The headline sits above the dome. The dome's width is between 44% and 48% of the window width, and its centre is within 40 pixels of the window's horizontal centre. `Add target` sits as a pill directly below the dome. The verdict card and target list form a right column. The sky chart and time bar sit below the dome in the centre column.

**Projects.**

- `/projects` shows one card per project: name, target, state, and total integration and owed time per filter. The cards form one column below 768 pixels, two columns from 768 to 1199, and three columns from 1200.
- `/projects/<id>` shows the project's name as the page heading, its state controls, the `Hours owed` table, the total integration region, the `Share` button, and a `Show ledger` button that slides up the ledger sheet. Below 768 pixels the sections stack. From 768 pixels the hours owed table and the total integration sit side by side.

**Sessions.** Below 768 pixels each session is a card. From 768 pixels the `Sessions` table sits on a lighter sheet that slides up over the dark ground. Columns: observing date, project, state, integration, frames.

**Library.** Below 768 pixels each calibration set is a card in the `Calibration library` list. From 768 pixels the same sets form the `Calibration library` table on the lighter sheet. Columns: name, kind, camera, gain, offset, exposure, temperature, binning, filter, age in days, state. When the imager has no sets, the empty-library copy appears over the sky disc with no targets.

**Site.** The site form, the horizon painter (a strip as wide as the content showing azimuth 0 to 360 degrees left to right and altitude 0 to 90 degrees bottom to top, with the 72 mask bins drawn as a filled treeline), the verdict card, the `New-moon weekend reminder` controls with the `Upcoming reminders` list, and the `Pushes` region with the `Push log` table. Below 768 pixels everything stacks. From 768 pixels the form and the painter sit side by side above the reminders and push log.

**Shared page `/s/<token>`.** The project name as the page heading, the `Integration summary` table (filter, planned, logged, owed), total integration, session count and the rounded site line. There is no navigation and no private controls.

At 375, 768 and 1280 pixels wide no page ever scrolls sideways.

### Interactions (DP-04, DP-12, DP-13, DP-14, DP-15, DP-17, DP-18, DP-22, DP-35, DP-56)

**Keyboard everywhere (DP-56).**

- Every control above is reachable with Tab in reading order and works with Enter or Space.
- When a dialog or sheet is open, Tab from its last control moves to its first, and Shift+Tab from its first moves to its last. Focus never reaches the page behind it.
- Escape closes the top dialog or sheet, and focus returns to the control that opened it.
- On the Plan screen, `[` and `]` move the night time handle 5 minutes earlier or later, and `A` opens `Add target to plan`. These work whatever control in the Plan screen has focus, including buttons and the dome. They do not fire while a text box or spin button has focus.
- On the Sessions screen, `N` opens `New session` under the same rule.

**Night time handle (DP-12).**

- Arrow Right and Arrow Up move it 5 minutes later; Arrow Left and Arrow Down 5 minutes earlier.
- Shift with Arrow Right or Arrow Up, and Page Up, move it 30 minutes later; Shift with Arrow Left or Arrow Down, and Page Down, move it 30 minutes earlier.
- Home goes to minute 0 (dusk) and End to the last minute (dawn).
- The two step buttons move it 30 minutes.
- Dragging the handle along the bar picks the minute under the pointer, rounded to the nearest whole minute. On release it snaps to the nearest multiple of 5.
- The handle never leaves 0 to `<night minutes>`. `<night minutes>` is the night's `minutes` from the API.
- The slider's value and value text change to the new minute as soon as the key is pressed or the drag is released.
- The page asks the server for the plan at that minute. When requests overlap, only the reply to the newest request is painted.

**Recompute in place (DP-13).** Moving the handle, changing `Minimum altitude` (saved on change, without a submit button) and adding or removing a target each repaint, from the server's reply and without navigation:

- the target list's altitudes, position statuses and usable minutes;
- the verdict card;
- hours owed after tonight;
- total integration.

Each action ends with exactly one announcement in `Plan updates`. Repaints that are not user actions (the 60-second refresh) update the figures silently and never replace the latest announcement.

**Sky chart and lens (DP-14).**

- The chart shows the sky as seen from the site: altitude rings at 0, 30 and 60 degrees, azimuth around the edge with north at the top, and the painted treeline as a dark silhouette rising from the edge. Each plan target has its night arc as a faint line and its dot at the handle's minute. The Moon is a small disc.
- A target dot whose position status is not clear is drawn at about a third of its normal brightness.
- A circular lens 160 pixels across follows the pointer over the chart. When a target in the list or the chart itself has keyboard focus, the lens centres on the focused target.
- Inside the lens the treeline is not drawn, so hidden arcs and dots show at full brightness.
- `Lens readout` names the target whose dot is nearest the lens centre and inside the lens, or says no target is under it.

**3D sky dome (DP-15).**

- Dragging on the dome with pointer or touch orbits the camera, with normal or reduced motion. Dragging right by 100 pixels adds 25 degrees to the azimuth and dragging left subtracts 25; dragging up by 100 pixels adds 25 degrees of elevation and dragging down subtracts 25.
- With the dome focused, Arrow Right adds 5 degrees to the azimuth and Arrow Left subtracts 5, Arrow Up adds 5 degrees of elevation and Arrow Down subtracts 5, `+` raises zoom by 0.1 and `-` lowers it by 0.1, and `0` resets the view.
- A mouse wheel notch away from the user raises zoom by 0.1 and a notch toward the user lowers it by 0.1. Spreading two fingers raises zoom and pinching them together lowers it, in proportion to the pinch.
- Double-click or double-tap resets the view to elevation 35, azimuth 180, zoom 1.0.
- Elevation is held between 5 and 85 degrees, and zoom between 0.6 and 2.0. Azimuth wraps from 359 to 0.
- The dome's accessible name shows the settled camera values and the handle minute within half a second of any change.

**Phone and touch (DP-18).** Touch drags on the time bar scrub, and touch drags on the dome orbit. A tap on the chart moves the lens there and pins it until the next tap. Every tappable control is at least 44 by 44 pixels.

**Dialogs that outlive timers (DP-22, DP-35).**

- The Plan screen repeats its latest plan request (same date and same handle minute) at least once every 60 seconds while open, and after every action. Other private screens re-read `GET /api/tonight` at the same rate. A refresh never moves the handle.
- A refresh that turns the conditions stale shows the stale banner but never closes an open `Sky conditions` dialog or resets the values typed into it.
- The session editor's notes box keeps its text and focus through lock expiry, a refused save and every refresh.
- A dialog that is reopened while its closing motion is still running stays open.

**Horizon painting (DP-04).**

- Pressing on the painter and dragging sets each bin the pointer crosses to the altitude under the pointer, rounded to a whole degree.
- With the painter focused, Arrow Left and Arrow Right select the previous or next 5-degree bin (wrapping), Arrow Up and Arrow Down change that bin by 1 degree, and Shift with Arrow Up or Down changes it by 5 degrees, always within 0 to 90.
- The painter saves the whole mask at the end of each drag, and half a second after the last key press. The verdict card on the Site screen then repaints from the reply.

### Visual design (DP-58, DP-59)

The direction is **glow on night**: the screen is a dark backyard with one cyan light, and the light falls only where the eye should go.

- **Colour.**
  - The ground is a near-black navy that runs edge to edge.
  - Cards are a deep blue-slate surface a step lighter than the ground.
  - Text is a pale blue-white ink. Secondary labels, axis numbers and timestamps are a grey-blue muted tone.
  - The single accent is a bright cyan. It appears only on the active time (the handle and the chart's minute line), the one primary action, the verdict and the focus ring.
  - Text on cyan is an almost-black navy.
  - There is no second accent, no red night-vision mode and no gradient other than the soft cyan glow behind the hero object.
  - Every pairing of text and background meets WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
- **Type.**
  - Sora is the display face and Sora is the body face.
  - Headings use Sora's light weight, large. Each heading level is about a third larger than the level below it. The Plan headline on wide screens is very large and light, about one sixteenth of the window width.
  - Labels are Sora semi-bold in sentence case, never tracked capitals.
  - Every number (minutes, degrees, durations, counts) uses Sora's tabular figures, so columns of numbers line up and ticking numerals do not jitter sideways.
- **Composition and hero.**
  - Each screen has one glowing key object on the full-bleed dark. On Plan and Unlock it is the sky disc or dome, centred, with the imager's painted treeline around its rim and a soft cyan glow behind it as the only light.
  - The headline sits above it and the single solid pill action below it.
  - Dense content (the sessions table, library table and ledger) sits on a lighter sheet that slides up over the dark, so reading tables never means reading pale text on black.
  - Density is comfortable: generous space between cards, table rows tall enough to tap.
- **Cards and controls.**
  - Plan, project, session and library panels are surface cards with a thin, faint white edge. The active card has a brighter edge.
  - The verdict card is the one bright card per screen, lit with the cyan glow along its edge.
  - Cards share one generous corner rounding. Inputs share a smaller rounding.
  - Only the single primary action on each screen is a full pill.
  - Every control is at least 44 pixels tall and wide.
- **Empty and error states.** Empty states show the sky disc with no targets and one plain sentence. Errors name the cause in a plain sentence next to the field or action, never an icon alone.
- **Treeline lens craft (DP-59).**
  - The dome reads as a real hemisphere with the zenith at the top, and its rim sits at the horizon.
  - The painted treeline rises from the rim as a dark, slightly irregular band that follows the 72 bins exactly.
  - Target tracks are glowing tubes in cyan-tinted white, brightest where the target clears the treeline.
  - The Moon is a sphere lit from the server's Sun direction, so its lit fraction matches the illumination figure.
  - Stars are fine points fixed to sidereal time.
  - The flat chart, the lens edge and the verdict numerals use the same cyan, so the dome, chart, lens and verdict read as one lit instrument.

### Motion and effects (DP-12, DP-15, DP-16, DP-17, DP-57)

General motion (DP-57):

- Hover, press, focus and toggle feedback (a slight brightening of a card's edge, the pill dipping in brightness on press) takes about a sixth of a second (0.16 s).
- Sheets slide up from the bottom and dialogs fade and rise slightly into place in about 0.42 seconds, always under half a second. They leave in the same time or less.
- Every motion starts quickly and settles softly at the end, with no bounce except the handle's settle.
- No element animates while the imager is not interacting: the glow is still, stars do not twinkle and nothing pulses. There are no decorative loops.

The signature, the **Treeline lens**, in observable steps:

1. The imager presses an arrow key or releases a drag on the night time handle.
2. On a key press the handle glides to the new minute in about a sixth of a second. On a drag release it settles on the snapped minute with a short spring that stops in under half a second and overshoots by no more than a few minutes' width.
3. The plan updates in a fixed order across under half a second, each step starting about a tenth of a second after the previous one:
   - **Position**: target dots slide along their arcs in the chart, and in the dome the star field turns and target dots move along their tracks.
   - **Mask clip**: dots passing behind the treeline dim, and dots coming out brighten.
   - **Verdict**: the verdict card's usable minutes tick to the new number.
   - **Hours owed**: the owed-after-tonight duration ticks to its new value.
4. Moving the handle back reverses the same steps in the same order. The final numbers always equal the server's reply.
5. The lens follows the pointer with no lag effect, and its readout changes when the nearest target changes.

The 3D dome (DP-15):

- It renders with three.js in WebGL. It shows the hemisphere, the horizon rim, the 3D treeline band built from the horizon mask, target track tubes, the lit Moon sphere and a star field fixed to the server's local sidereal time.
- Idle: no motion at all. With nothing touched and the handle still, two pictures of the dome taken seconds apart are identical.
- Orbit and zoom follow input as described in Interactions. With normal motion the camera eases to the new view in about a sixth of a second, with no drift after input stops.
- When the night time handle moves, the dome's star field and target dots move in step with the 2D chart, in the same update order.

Without WebGL (DP-16), the dome is replaced by a still picture of the 2D sky chart at the current minute. It carries the dome's accessible name pattern, with the elevation, azimuth and zoom left at their reset values. Beside it is the unavailable sentence. Every figure, verdict and announcement is identical to the WebGL path.

Reduced motion (DP-17), when the device asks for reduced motion:

- The handle jumps to the key step or the release point in one frame, with no spring.
- Orbit and handle changes jump the dome to the end view in one frame, with no easing.
- Numerals change to the final value at once.
- Sheets and dialogs appear and disappear without movement.
- The lens no longer follows the pointer. A click or tap on the chart pins it at that point, and the readout updates.
- Dragging still orbits the dome. All values, verdicts and announcements are identical.

## Backend

### API (DP-01, DP-02, DP-03, DP-06, DP-07, DP-08, DP-20, DP-24, DP-26, DP-29, DP-32, DP-33, DP-34, DP-37, DP-38, DP-39, DP-41, DP-43, DP-44, DP-48, DP-49, DP-51, DP-53, DP-54)

**General rules.**

- **Content types.** Request and response bodies are JSON (`application/json`) except frame and master uploads and downloads.
- **Body limits.** JSON bodies may be at most 65,536 bytes (exactly 65,536 is allowed). Frame and master upload bodies may be 1 to 20,000,000 bytes (exactly 20,000,000 is allowed).
- **Unknown fields** in a request body are ignored.
- **Time fields.**
  - Night events (dusk, dawn, hour starts, window starts, reminder times) are ISO 8601 in site-local time with the site's UTC offset at that instant, in whole minutes, for example `2026-11-03T18:12:00+00:00`.
  - Record timestamps (`created_at`, `entered_at`, `sent_at`, `server_time`) are UTC with `Z`.
- **Error body.** Every error has one shape: `{"error":{"code":"<code>","message":"<plain sentence>"}}`. A 422 adds `"fields":{"<field>":"<message>"}`.
- **Authentication.** Every `/api` route except `POST /api/workspaces`, `POST /api/unlock`, `GET /api/shared/<token>` and `GET /api/health` needs the workspace key, either as `Authorization: Bearer <key>` or as the `darkframe_key` cookie set by unlock.
- **Ids** are server-generated strings.

**Error precedence**, the same for every route. The first rule that applies decides the response:

1. 413 `too_large`
2. 415 `unsupported_media_type`
3. 401 `unauthenticated` (message `Unlock your workspace first.`)
4. 404 `not_found` for an unknown id or one owned by another imager, anywhere in the path or body
5. a matching idempotent replay returns its stored response
6. 409 state conflicts
7. 422 `validation`

Rows below list only their own extra errors. When an internal service a route needs does not answer within 5 seconds, the route answers 503 `service_unavailable` with message `<service name> is not answering. Try again in a moment.`, where `<service name>` is `The sky engine`, `The ledger` or `The push dispatcher`.

### Business rules (DP-02, DP-06, DP-07, DP-08, DP-09, DP-21, DP-22, DP-24, DP-25, DP-26, DP-32, DP-33, DP-34, DP-37, DP-39, DP-41, DP-43, DP-44, DP-48, DP-49, DP-51, DP-52, DP-53)

**Ownership (DP-02).** Every record belongs to exactly one imager. A request that names another imager's project, session, batch, frame, calibration set, version or plan target, whether in the path or in a body field such as `project_id` or `calibration_set_id`, answers 404 `not_found`, exactly as for an id that does not exist. It never answers 409 or 422.

**Observing night (DP-09, DP-25).**

- An instant belongs to the observing date that is its site-local date when its site-local time is 12:00 or later, and the previous site-local date when it is earlier than 12:00. The browser's and server's own time zones never matter.
- "Tonight" is the observing date of the server's current instant.
- The current minute is the number of whole minutes since dusk, held to 0 before dusk and to `minutes` after dawn.
- The device clock may be wrong. The browser shows the date, minute, verdict and countdown the server returns and never derives them from its own clock.

**Astronomical night and positions (DP-06).**

- **Dusk** is the first whole UTC minute after 12:00 site-local on the observing date at which the Sun's altitude is below -18 degrees.
- **Dawn** is the first whole minute after dusk at which it is -18 degrees or higher.
- `minutes` is dawn minus dusk.
- Night minute `m` is the instant dusk plus `m` minutes.
- When the Sun never gets below -18 degrees between that noon and the next noon, there is no astronomical night: `minutes` is 0, minute 0 is the site-local midnight after the observing date, and only minute 0 is valid.
- Every filter window is half-open: it holds the whole-minute instants from its start minute up to but not including its end minute. Broadband filters (`L`, `R`, `G`, `B`) use start 0 and end `minutes`. The narrowband window for `Ha`, `OIII` and `SII` opens 30 minutes before dusk and closes 30 minutes after dawn: start -30 and end `minutes` plus 30. When `minutes` is 0 no filter has any instant.

Method:

- Sun and Moon positions use the low-precision formulas of the Astronomical Almanac.
- Greenwich mean sidereal time uses the standard IAU 1982 expression.
- Target J2000 right ascension and declination are precessed to the date.
- Nutation, aberration and atmospheric refraction are ignored.
- The Moon gets topocentric parallax from the site's latitude, longitude and elevation.
- Azimuth runs from north through east.

Rounding:

- Altitudes, azimuths, separations and the sidereal time are rounded to 0.1 degree, half away from zero.
- Moon illumination (the lit fraction of the disc) is rounded to 2 decimals.

Figures must agree with a precise ephemeris within 0.2 degrees for the Sun and targets, 0.5 degrees for the Moon, 0.02 for illumination and 2 minutes for dusk and dawn.

**Horizon mask.** The mask holds 72 bins. Bin `i` covers azimuths from `5×i` up to but not including `5×i + 5` degrees, and its value is the treeline altitude in that range.

**Position status at an instant:**

1. `below-horizon` when the altitude is below 0.
2. Otherwise `behind-treeline` when the altitude is below the mask altitude of its bin and that mask altitude is at least the minimum altitude.
3. Otherwise `below-minimum` when the altitude is below the minimum altitude.
4. Otherwise `clear`.

**Sky conditions (DP-20, DP-21).**

- A night's `hours` are the site-local hour starts from the hour containing the narrowband window's start to the hour containing its last minute. Hours are distinct instants, so a repeated local hour on a clock-change night appears twice with different offsets.
- An instant uses the entry whose hour start is at or before it and less than one hour before it.
- An instant is **sky-clear** when that entry exists with `cloud_pct` of 30 or less and `transparency` of 3 or more. `seeing` is stored and shown but never used.
- Saving conditions sets `entered_at` to the server's now.
- Conditions are `stale` when the server's now is more than `CONDITIONS_STALE_SECONDS` seconds after `entered_at`. The default is 10800 seconds (3 hours).
- Stale conditions still count toward the verdict. Only the flag and the banner change.

**Moon rule.** An instant is Moon-clear for a filter when any of these holds:

- the Moon's topocentric altitude is below 0;
- the illumination is below 0.25;
- the target's separation from the Moon is at least the filter's minimum: 60 degrees for `L`, `R`, `G` and `B`, 45 degrees for `OIII`, 30 degrees for `Ha` and `SII`.

**Usable minutes and verdict (DP-07, DP-08).**

- Only targets whose project is `active` count (`"counts":true`). A target whose project later leaves `active` stays listed with `"counts":false`.
- For a counting target and a filter with a planned allotment, `usable_minutes` counts whole-minute instants at which the target is `clear`, Moon-clear for that filter and sky-clear. The counted instants start at the filter window's start when `m` is 0, so at -30 for `Ha`, `OIII` and `SII` and at 0 for `L`, `R`, `G` and `B`. When `m` is above 0 they start at `m`. In both cases they run up to but not including the window's end. The narrowband leading margin therefore counts only when `m` is 0.
- `clear_minutes` is the number of whole-minute instants from minute `m` up to but not including `minutes` at which the target's position status is `clear`, ignoring the Moon and the sky.
- `owed_after_tonight_seconds` is the larger of 0 and `owed_seconds` minus 60 times `usable_minutes`.
- `best` is the counting target and filter with `owed_seconds` above 0 that has the most usable minutes. Ties go to the earliest-added target, then to the filter order `L R G B Ha OIII SII`.
- Targets are evaluated independently. Overlapping imaging time is not shared out.
- The verdict follows the first rule that matches:
  1. `no-go` with reason `no-astronomical-night` when `minutes` is 0.
  2. `unknown` with reason `no-conditions` when the night has no condition entries.
  3. `no-go` with reason `no-targets` when there is no counting target with an owed filter.
  4. `go` when best has 60 or more usable minutes.
  5. Otherwise `no-go` with reason `under-60-minutes`.
- `clear_window` is the first unbroken run of best's usable instants among the instants counted for best's `usable_minutes`, so it starts no earlier than where that count starts. `start_minute` is inclusive and `end_minute` exclusive. `start` and `end` are their site-local times. `start_minute` is negative (from -30 to -1) only when `m` is 0, best's filter is narrowband and the run begins before dusk. In every other case it is at least `m`. `end_minute` may exceed `minutes` by up to 30 for a narrowband best.
- `is_tonight` is true when the plan's date is the server's current observing date, for `GET /api/tonight` and for `GET /api/plans/<date>?minute=<m>` at any minute. `starts_in_minutes` is `null` unless `is_tonight` is true and `clear_window` is not null. Otherwise it is the whole number of minutes from the server's now to the window's start, rounded down and never below 0, and it does not depend on the handle.
- `total_integration_seconds` is the sum of logged seconds of the counting targets' projects.
- A night's verdict for pushes is the verdict at minute 0.
- The verdict is decided only on the server.

**Card figures and countdown refresh (DP-22).** The verdict card is painted from one plan body as follows:
- `<n>` in `<n> usable minutes` is best's `usable_minutes`, or 0 when `best` is null.
- Owed after tonight is the sum of `owed_after_tonight_seconds` over every filter of every counting target in the plan, or 0 when there is none, shown in the duration format.
- `<p>` comes from `moon.illumination`, and the verdict word and best line from `verdict` and `best`.
- The countdown uses the tonight form when `is_tonight` is true: `No clear window tonight` when `clear_window` is null, `Clear window opens in <h> h <mm> min` when `starts_in_minutes` is above 0, and `Clear window open until <HH:MM>` (the window's end in site time) when it is 0. When `is_tonight` is false it uses the other-nights form from `clear_window`'s start and end, or `No clear window`.

The Plan screen's card changes on every action that returns a plan body and on its 60-second refresh. That refresh repeats the latest plan request, so a handle moved to another minute keeps that minute and only the server's now moves the countdown. Other private screens re-read `GET /api/tonight` at least once every 60 seconds while open.

**Project states (DP-43).**

| From | To | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| `draft` | `active` | at least one filter has `planned_seconds` above 0, else 409 `no_allotments` |
| `draft` | `archived` | none |
| `active` | `paused` | none |
| `paused` | `active` | none |
| `active` | `complete` | every filter's `owed_seconds` is 0 or less, else 409 `hours_still_owed` |
| `complete` | `archived` | none |

Every other pair, including same-state moves and anything out of `archived`, answers 409 `illegal_transition` with message `A project that is <from> cannot become <to>.` Allotments and edits are refused with 409 `project_closed` for `complete` and `archived` projects. Plans accept and new sessions start only for `active` projects (409 `project_not_active`).

**Ledger (DP-44, DP-32, DP-33).** Each project and filter has three accounts: `allotment`, `owed` and `integrated`. Every movement writes exactly two entries in whole seconds whose sum is 0:

- **Allot.** Setting `planned_seconds` from P to Q posts `owed` +(Q−P) and `allotment` −(Q−P). An unchanged value posts nothing.
- **Log.** Logging a batch of integration E (sub count times exposure) posts `owed` −E and `integrated` +E, one movement per batch.
- **Reversal.** A reversal posts the exact negation of both entries of the movement it reverses and names it in `reverses_movement_id`. Entries are never edited or deleted.

For each filter, `planned_seconds` is minus the sum of `allotment` entries, `logged_seconds` is the sum of `integrated` entries, and `owed_seconds` is the sum of `owed` entries, which always equals planned minus logged. These figures are computed only from the entries.

**Session states (DP-24).**

| From | To | Action | Ledger |
|---|---|---|---|
| `planned` | `imaging` | start | none |
| `imaging` | `logged` | log, needs at least one batch (else 409 `no_batches`) | one log movement per batch |
| `logged` or `amended` | `amended` | amend | see below |
| `planned`, `logged` or `amended` | `trashed` | trash | reverses every log movement of the session that has not been reversed |
| `trashed` | the state before the trash | restore | for a `logged` or `amended` session, a new log movement per batch equal to its integration |

Every other pair answers 409 `illegal_transition` with message `A session that is <from> cannot become <to>.`

- Batches are added only while `imaging`, otherwise 409 `wrong_state`.
- **Amending** compares the new batch list with the current one:
  - a batch whose fields are unchanged posts nothing;
  - a changed batch posts a reversal of its latest unreversed log movement and a new log movement;
  - a batch left out is removed with a reversal, but a batch with frames cannot be removed (409 `batch_has_frames`);
  - a new batch posts a log movement.
- Amending a batch's camera, gain, offset, exposure, temperature, binning or filter drops any calibration link that no longer matches.
- **Restore** brings back every field of the session body exactly as it was before the trash, apart from `state` and `previous_state`: notes, batches, batch ids, frames, calibration links, dates and integration. Frames stay in storage while the session is trashed.
- When two state changes on one session arrive together, the first one committed wins and the other answers 409 `illegal_transition`.

**Observing date of a session (DP-25).** Creating a session stores the given `observing_date`. Starting it replaces that date with the observing date of `started_at` under the noon rule. For example, a start at 23:30 or at 01:10 site time belongs to the evening that began before midnight.

**Batch idempotency (DP-26).**

- `Idempotency-Key` values are kept per imager for 24 hours.
- A submit whose key and JSON body match an earlier submit returns that submit's stored status and body and creates nothing.
- The same key with a different body answers 409 `idempotency_conflict`.
- Of two simultaneous submits with the same key and body, exactly one creates the batch and both receive the same response.

**Frames (DP-29).**

- The server writes the bytes to object storage, reads back the stored object's size and checksum, and only when they match the upload does it record the frame and increase `frame_count`.
- When storage fails or the check differs, nothing is recorded and the 503 from API is returned.
- A frame name may appear once per batch.

**Edit lock (DP-34, DP-35).**

- A lock lasts `DRAFT_LOCK_SECONDS` seconds from when it is taken or renewed. The default is 900 seconds (15 minutes).
- A lock request without a token, or with an expired one, succeeds only when no other unexpired lock exists, otherwise 409 `session_locked`. Of two simultaneous requests, exactly one wins.
- A notes save needs the current unexpired token. An expired token answers 409 `lock_expired`; a token replaced by another device's lock answers 409 `session_locked`.
- On `lock_expired` the editor takes a new lock and repeats the save once. On `session_locked` it keeps the text and shows the held copy.

**Calibration states (DP-37).** The stored states are `building`, `ready` and `retired`. Allowed edges:

- `building` to `ready`, only when a master is recorded, else 409 `master_missing`;
- `building`, `ready` or stale to `retired`.

Every other pair answers 409 `illegal_transition` with message `A calibration set that is <from> cannot become <to>.`

A `ready` set is reported as `stale` when `age_days` exceeds `max_age_days`. `age_days` is the site-local today minus `captured_on`, in days. `max_age_days` is 180 for darks, 30 for flats and 365 for bias. An age equal to the maximum is still ready. A stale set still calibrates, with `"warning":"stale"`. A building or retired set never calibrates: 409 `calibration_not_usable`.

**Matching (DP-39).**

- Fields compared, by kind:
  - darks: camera, binning, gain, offset, exposure and temperature;
  - bias: camera, binning, gain and offset;
  - flats: camera, binning and filter.
- Camera names compare exactly.
- Temperatures match when they differ by 2.0 degrees Celsius or less.
- A batch holds at most one set per kind. Linking a new set of a kind replaces the old one.
- The refusal message is `Calibration refused: ` followed by every failing reason in the order camera, binning, gain, offset, exposure, temperature, filter, joined by `; ` and ending with `.`. Each reason reads:
  - `camera <set> does not match <batch>`
  - `binning <set> does not match <batch>`
  - `gain <set> does not match <batch>`
  - `offset <set> does not match <batch>`
  - `exposure <set> s does not match <batch> s`
  - `temperature <set> C is more than 2.0 C from <batch> C`, with temperatures to one decimal
  - `filter <set> does not match <batch>`
- For example: `Calibration refused: gain 120 does not match 100; temperature -5.0 C is more than 2.0 C from -10.0 C.`
- `mismatches` lists the same fields in the same order.

**Calibration versions (DP-41).**

- Creation is version 1.
- Each accepted PATCH, master upload and restore adds one version holding every editable field and the master reference.
- Restoring version `n` copies `n`'s fields and master reference exactly into a new version. Master objects are never overwritten.

**Share links (DP-48, DP-49).**

- A project has at most one live link.
- Creating a new link kills the previous one at once, and revoking kills the current one at once.
- A link stops working at its `expires_at`.
- Shared coordinates are rounded to the nearest multiple of 0.5 degrees, halves away from zero, written with one decimal, and negative zero is written `0.0`. For example, with the seed data at start, Ines Okafor's site shares as 52.0, 0.0.
- Notes, exact coordinates, elevation, site label, horizon, sessions, frames and calibration never appear on the shared page or in its API.

**Pushes (DP-51, DP-52).**

- After any change that can move a night's verdict (conditions, plan targets, minimum altitude, horizon, site, allotments, logs, amendments, trash, restore, project state), the sky engine re-evaluates the imager's affected plans within 2 seconds.
- When a night's minute-0 verdict becomes `go` or `no-go` and differs from the last verdict recorded for that night (including the first evaluation and a change from `unknown`), the sky engine writes one row to its durable outbox. A change to `unknown` writes no row but is recorded, so a later return to `go` pushes again.
- The push dispatcher takes each outbox row once and creates one push with status `queued`. It publishes it to the imager's topic within 10 seconds when ntfy answers, and marks it `sent` with `sent_at` only after ntfy answers with a 2xx status.
- A failed attempt marks the push `unsent`, increases `attempts` and retries every `PUSH_RETRY_SECONDS` seconds (default 10), indefinitely, until ntfy accepts it.
- Each push produces exactly one ntfy message, including when the dispatcher process stops and starts again. While the dispatcher is down, rows wait in the outbox and are delivered exactly once when it returns, with no gateway restart.
- When the imager has no `push_topic`, no push is created.
- Push titles and messages:
  - go: title `Darkframe: go for <date>`, message `<site label> near <lat>, <lon>: <target name> in <filter>, clear window <HH:MM>-<HH:MM>, <n> usable minutes.`
  - no-go: title `Darkframe: no-go for <date>`, message `<site label> near <lat>, <lon>: no clear window of 60 minutes.`
  - In both, `<lat>` and `<lon>` are the share-grid values. Notes and exact coordinates never appear in any push.

**New-moon weekend reminder (DP-53).**

- New moons are computed from the standard mean new-moon series with its main periodic corrections, to the minute (within 2 minutes of a precise value).
- An occurrence's `new_moon_local_date` is the site-local date of the new moon. `weekend_saturday` is the Saturday nearest that date, within 3 days either way. The reminder fires on the Friday before that Saturday at `local_time` in the site's time zone.
- If that wall time does not exist (spring clock change), it moves forward by the length of the gap. For example, 01:30 on a night when 01:00 jumps to 02:00 becomes 02:30.
- If it occurs twice (autumn clock change), the first, earlier instant is used.
- The wall time stays the same across clock changes, so its UTC instant shifts by the offset change.
- `occurrences` lists the first `count` new moons at or after 00:00 site-local on `from`.
- While enabled, the push dispatcher creates one `reminder` push per occurrence whose `remind_utc` has passed since the reminder was enabled, with title `Darkframe: new-moon weekend` and message `New Moon <new moon local date>. Dark weekend from <site label> starts Saturday <weekend saturday>.`

### Services (DP-29, DP-38, DP-44, DP-51, DP-52, DP-54, DP-60)

**PostgreSQL** holds every record.

- Each service owns one schema: `ledger`, `sky_engine` and `push_dispatcher`. The gateway stores only workspace keys, in schema `gateway`.
- No service reads another's tables. The one exception is that the push dispatcher reads and marks rows of the sky engine's outbox table `sky_engine.verdict_outbox`.
- Ledger entries live in `ledger.entries`, with at least the columns `movement_id`, `project_id`, `filter`, `account` and `seconds`. For every `movement_id` the `seconds` sum to 0.
- Schemas, tables and seed data are applied idempotently every time `serve` starts, so an empty database is ready without a separate build step.

**MinIO** holds frames and calibration masters in the bucket `S3_BUCKET`.

- Frame key: `frames/<imager_id>/<session_id>/<batch_id>/<frame_id>/<name>`.
- Master key: `calibration/<imager_id>/<set_id>/v<version>/<filename>`.
- Objects are stored with the upload's content type, and their bytes equal the upload exactly.
- When MinIO is unreachable, uploads answer the 503s from API and nothing is counted. Every other screen keeps working.

**ntfy** receives each push as one message on the topic `<push_topic>`, published to `NTFY_URL` with basic authentication using `NTFY_USERNAME` and `NTFY_PASSWORD`. The message carries the push's title as its title, its message as the body, and the tag `telescope`. When ntfy is unreachable, pushes show the unsent state from Feedback states and retry as Business rules state. Plans, logs, the library and health keep answering normally.

## Technical requirements

### Deployment (DP-54)

- Python 3.12 and pip are available. No other language runtime is guaranteed, so browser code ships as files the server serves directly.
- The app is installed and run as an unprivileged user. Dependencies go into a `.venv` inside `/app`, and the manifest calls its tools by path (`.venv/bin/<tool>`) or as `python -m <tool>` through `.venv/bin/python`.
- `/app/run.json` holds string `install`, `build` and `serve` commands and `"_schema":"run-manifest-v1"`, for example:

```
{"_schema":"run-manifest-v1",
 "install":"python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt",
 "build":".venv/bin/python -m darkframe_build",
 "serve":".venv/bin/python -m darkframe_serve"}
```

- The single `serve` command starts four separate operating-system processes: gateway, sky engine, ledger and push dispatcher.
- Only the gateway listens publicly, on `0.0.0.0` at `APP_PUBLIC_PORT` (4173 when unset). The internal services listen on `127.0.0.1`:
  - sky engine at `APP_PUBLIC_PORT` + 100;
  - ledger at `APP_PUBLIC_PORT` + 200;
  - push dispatcher at `APP_PUBLIC_PORT` + 300.
- Each internal service answers `GET /internal/health` on its own port with `200 {"name":"<service name>","version":"1.0.0","status":"ok"}`, using the names from the health shape.
- The gateway never exposes `/internal` paths: they answer 404 on the public port.
- `GET /api/health` follows the API shape and answers 200 with `"status":"ok"` once the gateway is up with the sky engine, ledger and PostgreSQL.

### Configuration and secrets (DP-55, DP-60)

Read at start, from the environment only:

- `APP_PUBLIC_PORT`
- `DATABASE_URL`
- `S3_ENDPOINT`, `S3_REGION`, `S3_BUCKET`, `S3_FORCE_PATH_STYLE`, `S3_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `S3_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`
- `NTFY_URL`, `NTFY_USERNAME`, `NTFY_PASSWORD`
- `CONDITIONS_STALE_SECONDS`, `DRAFT_LOCK_SECONDS`, `PUSH_RETRY_SECONDS`, with the defaults in Business rules

No credential appears in source, in any served file or in any response. The browser never contacts PostgreSQL, MinIO or ntfy; every file and push goes through the server.

### Server boundary (DP-55)

- The app is same-origin only. It sends no cross-origin permission headers. A state-changing request whose `Origin` header names another origin answers 403 `{"error":{"code":"cross_origin","message":"Requests must come from Darkframe itself."}}`.
- Body limits and content types are as API states: 413 `too_large` above a limit, and 415 `unsupported_media_type` for a JSON route sent another content type.
- The server serves only the built browser files, the Sora font files, the three.js file and the API. Requests for source files, `run.json`, `.env`, `.venv`, `.git` or any path outside the built files answer 404.
- The workspace key cookie is HttpOnly.
- A dependency unreachable at boot never stops `serve`.
  - With ntfy down, everything except delivery works, and pushes deliver once it answers, with no restart.
  - With MinIO down, uploads answer 503 and everything else works.
  - With PostgreSQL down, health answers 503, and the app recovers within 10 seconds of PostgreSQL answering, with no restart.

### Browser support and performance (DP-46, DP-56)

- Current Chromium and WebKit, at 375x812, 768x1024 and 1280x800, with touch and reduced-motion variants.
- Every screen is usable within three seconds of navigation on the seed data.
- A plan evaluation for up to 8 targets answers within one second, so a handle step repaints within about a second.

## Working rules

- IF-01: `/app/run.json` exists with string `install`, `build` and `serve` fields and `"_schema":"run-manifest-v1"`, and `serve` alone starts all four services.
- IF-02: `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` at `/app` lists every shipped package with its licence. Shipped packages means the production dependency tree: the packages `install` puts into `/app/.venv`, as `.venv/bin/python -m pip list --format json` reports them, plus every vendored browser file (three.js, Sora).
- IF-03: Shipped packages use only these licences: MIT, BSD-2-Clause, BSD-3-Clause, Apache-2.0, ISC, PSF-2.0, or OFL-1.1 for fonts.
- IF-04: No datastore other than the provided PostgreSQL, MinIO and ntfy: no SQLite files, no Redis, no file-based records outside MinIO.
- IF-05: All browser assets, including three.js and the Sora fonts, are served from the app's own origin. No CDN or third-party URL appears in any served page.
- IF-06: Python dependencies live in `/app/.venv`, and the manifest calls tools through `.venv/bin/` or `python -m`.
- IF-07: The provided development service definitions and their credentials stay untouched.

## Engineering standards

- The code is idiomatic Python 3.12 with type hints on public functions. Browser code is plain modules without dead imports.
- Names in code match this brief's domain terms: observing date, astronomical night, horizon mask, usable minutes, verdict, allotment, owed, integrated, batch, calibration set, master, push.
- Each domain rule is decided in exactly one place in the source: each state machine's transitions, the noon rule, usable minutes, the verdict, owed seconds, calibration matching, staleness, coordinate rounding and push titles.
- No abstraction has only a single implementation, and there are no defensive checks for states the rules make impossible.
- There is no dead, commented-out or unreachable code, and no feature beyond this brief.
- Every error leaves the API in the one error shape, with the codes this brief names.
- Services talk only over their HTTP APIs and the outbox, and each touches only its own schema.
